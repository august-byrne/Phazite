# Phazite's presets

The presets that come with Phazite (`resources/presets/<device>/<kind>/<name>.phazitepreset`) and the drum sampler's kits, whose sounds Phazite synthesizes as it needs them ([design section 9](design.md#9-devices-containers-and-presets)). This page records what each device's presets cover, how classic instruments are recreated and named, how the presets are leveled, and how to check them. The pass of 2026-11-08 compared every instrument's presets with what the leading instruments ship and added the classic sounds producers expect; the pass of 2026-11-10 made the sounds of the example song Exodus, and what dark synthwave needs, from its measured original.

The kinds follow what Ableton Live, Logic, Bitwig and Arturia sort their factory sounds into: brass, strings, mallets, winds and percussion have kinds of their own rather than sitting under Lead, Pad or Pluck. Each preset's name is its own across the instruments (the FM synth's Brass and the synth's Poly Brass, not two "Brass"), so that a search finds one thing by a name.

**Conventions:**
- **A preset stands on its own,** as what its name says, and is never made for a song (the owner, 2026-11-09: "i want all of the presets to stand on their own two feet ... rather than catering to example songs that might be swapped out whenever i feel like it"). The example songs play presets as they come and mix them: their levels, sends, sidechains and the master's chain, not a preset tuned to the song or a patch the song builds in code.
- **A bass sounds at the note played**, as on Phazite's other instruments and in the piano roll; a sub an octave down is part of the sound where the preset has one. The DX7 transposes some of its basses an octave down; their FM presets here have their ratios doubled instead.
- **Velocity changes brightness, not only level,** where a played instrument does: plucks, keys, brass and basses close their filter for softer notes (the synth's Velocity to Cutoff or to a table's position, its base lowered so that a note at velocity 100 sounds as without it; the FM synth's and the sampler's Filter Velocity), about a quarter of an octave darker at velocity 50 than at 100 (the preset report's "Vel oct"). Not where the original doesn't: organs and the harpsichord, whose keys don't change their tone, sine basses, which have no tone to change, Square Pluck and Darksynth Bass, whose originals' velocity changes only their level, and the FM presets, whose modulators follow velocity as their originals' do (the Genesis's chip has no velocity); of these, Squelch Bass, Muted Pluck and Reed Lead close their filter for softer notes as well, about a quarter of an octave darker at velocity 50.
- **The mod wheel brings in vibrato** on the leads, brass and strings that don't use their LFO otherwise: on the synth, a slot from LFO 1 to the pitch at no amount, which a Mod Wheel slot on that amount raises; on the FM synth, its LFO on at no depth, to the pitch, and the wheel adds to its depth, as on the synths these come from.
- **Every note on a voice of its own** (2026-11-10), in poly mode: a key played again lets the last note's tail ring under it, so a pluck, a bass or a pad played as repeated notes rings into itself for as long as its release lasts. A preset that should cut its own tail, a sub or an 808 played as a line, is Mono.
- **Presets are dry:** no reverb or delay inside them, which users of other libraries prefer (they turn off on-board effects first). Phazite's own effects follow the instrument.

## Naming classic instruments

The presets recreate classic instruments' sounds, but are named by their sound, not by a trademark (the research of 2026-11-08, from the USPTO's records and how other software names its presets; this isn't legal advice):

- **808 and 909** name sounds and kits ("808 Kit", "909 Ride"), as Ableton names its "Kit-Core 808" and "909 Core Kit": no registration for "808" or "909" alone was found, and dictionaries define "808" as the sound. **"TR-808", "TR-909", "TB-303" and "Roland" are never used**: Roland registered them in 2020 and 2021 for plug-ins and sound recordings, and enforces them.
- **Every other classic gets a name for its sound**, as Ableton, Logic and Bitwig name theirs: Acid Bass (after the TB-303), DCO Bass and Strings and PWM Pad (Juno), Analog Brass (Jupiter), Poly Brass (OB-X), Poly Pad (Prophet), Ladder Bass and Fat Lead (Minimoog), Pedal Bass (Taurus), Mono Bass (SH-101), String Machine (Solina), Cinematic Brass (CS-80), Scream Lead (MS-20), Duo Lead (Odyssey), Hoover (Alpha Juno), Tine E-Piano (the DX7's E.PIANO 1), Lately Bass (the TX81Z's LatelyBass, a patch's name), 16-Bit Bass and Lead (the Genesis), House Organ (the M1's Organ 2), Rhythm Box Kit (the CR-78). Near-variants of a mark ("Moogy", "Wurli") aren't used either: Moog had Cherry Audio rename its "Minimode".
- **This page names the originals** in plain sentences, to say what a preset is after. The About box and `NOTICES.txt` say that other names of products are their owners' trademarks, named only to say what Phazite works with or what a sound is like, that Phazite isn't affiliated with their owners, and that its presets hold no recordings of their instruments (`resources/licenses/notices.json`).

## The classic sounds and where their settings come from

**Drum machines** (the drum sampler's kits, synthesized in `src/devices/drums/DrumMachines.cpp`):
- **808 Kit:** the TR-808's sixteen voices, General MIDI's drums where it has them (kick, rim shot, snare, clap, toms, hats, cymbal) and its own where it hasn't (claves for the second snare, maracas for the pedal hat, its three congas between the toms, its cowbell for the ride). Modeled on Werner, Abel and Smith's analyses of its bass drum (DAFx-14) and cymbal (ICMC 2014), the service notes' chart of each voice's pitch and decay, and published fits to the schematic: the kick a resonator at 50 Hz, its first 3.7 ms three times as fast, then a sigh from 23 % above; the snare's resonators at 238 and 476 Hz with noise from 2.75 to 5 kHz; toms at 90, 135 and 185 Hz and congas at 185, 280 and 400 Hz, each as short as the chart says; the hats, cymbal and cowbell from its six square oscillators (205.3 to 800 Hz) through its bands (7.1 and 3.44 kHz; the cowbell's 890 Hz), with no noise in them, as the circuit has none. Its kick cuts itself (Cut Itself), as the machine's one bass drum circuit starts again with each hit.
- **909 Kit:** the TR-909's analog kick (a rounded triangle swept down to 52 Hz), snare (two triangles 1.585 apart and noise that holds, then falls), toms (three partials, 1 : 1.5 : 2.75), rim shot and clap, and its sampled hats, crash and ride as dense metal at six bits, read back at 30 kHz as its converter reads them; a snappier second snare, a pedal hat, and each tom tuned low and high.
- **Rhythm Box Kit:** the CR-78's fourteen voices and its open hat: its small kick, its hats and cymbal of ringing band-passed noise with its metallic beat (three squares through a band-pass at 5.6 kHz), its guiro's scrape, maracas, tambourine, cowbell, claves, bongos and conga. Little is published about its circuits; these follow what is.
- **Trap Kit:** what producers expect of a trap kit: a short kick over a long 808 (a sine falling an octave onto G1 over 22 ms, saturated), which cuts itself (Cut Itself) so that its notes don't pile up, a crisp snare, a layered clap, tight hats for rolls, a snap, toms, a saturated 808 cowbell as phonk plays it, a shaker, a tambourine and a crash.
- **Percussion Kit:** hand percussion, as Bitwig and FL Studio ship it: congas (open and slapped), bongos, timbales, agogo bells, woodblocks, claves, a shaker, a cabasa, a tambourine and a triangle.
- **Synthwave Kit** (`src/devices/drums/FactoryDrums.cpp`, from `DrumSynthesis.h`): the 80s' cheap digital drum machines as synthwave plays them, after what its producers, its tutorials and its sample packs say defines it (the research of 2026-11-09: Attack Magazine's and ModeAudio's synthwave drum guides, The Midnight's and Timecop1983's set-ups, the AMS RMX16's manual, the SDS-V's circuit; the Ableton, Sample Magic and TR-707 Synthwave packs). A LinnDrum's or an Oberheim DMX's punchy kick over a sub (a sine falling from 190 to 52 Hz, a wooden knock and a click, clipped a little), snare (175 and 320 Hz under its snares' noise from 1.5 to 9 kHz), clap (the 808's four bursts 9 ms apart, at 1.1 kHz) and rim shot (480 Hz and 1.6 kHz), each as 8-bit mu-law samples at 28 kHz, as those machines held theirs. The gated reverb of the 80s on the snares, the clap and the toms, the genre's signature: a dense, bright room that comes in over 12 ms, holds 150 to 280 ms while falling 3 dB, and is shut in 50 ms whatever the drum's own decay, as the RMX16's Nonlin programs and Hugh Padgham's gated room did (`addGatedReverb()`), 6 to 12 dB under the hit. The Simmons SDS-V's second snare and toms (`makeSimmons()`): a triangle bending down, the toms from about an octave above to 70 to 175 Hz, with noise through a four-pole low-pass sweeping down without resonance, as the SDS-V's toms have none, and a click. And the 909's and the 707's 6-bit hats and cymbals at 25 kHz, their envelope after their grit as their VCAs put it, the open hat choked by the closed and the pedal. Left out, as what reads generic 80s rather than synthwave: congas, cabasa and a booming 808 or DMX kick, and resonant "pew" toms.
- **Dark Synthwave Kit:** the natural, dark drums of darksynth and outrun, each sound fitted on Phazite's own renders to a measured drum of the kind (the drums of the example song Exodus, by XCALIBUR, CC BY), clean and full band. A short, boomed kick: a sine falling from 410 to 80 Hz in its first milliseconds, swelling in over 9 ms, held 50 ms and cut, its body through a low bell and shelf of its own, the boom an engineer's EQ gives a kick. A low snare at 148 and 170 Hz with further shell modes; a clap of five strikes through a narrow band at 1.19 kHz, a little wide; a struck-metal perc on 37, where General MIDI has its rim shot. Each rings in a room that isn't gated (`addRoom()`): dry in the middle as it is hit, then wide and darker as it falls. A flat, bright open hat held 270 ms and cut; closed hat, pedal hat and crash fitted to their measured originals; synth toms by one rule fitted to three; a second snare and a ride made in the kit's character. Balanced at the quiet ends of the rule: the snares and the clap 5 LU under the kick, the perc 8, the toms 6.5, the open hat 8, the closed hats 9.5 and the cymbals 10.5; under its kick, Sidechain Pump ducks a sound 10.5 dB.

**The sidechain presets** (the compressor's Duck mode, keyed by another track: [design section 9](design.md#9-devices-containers-and-presets); since 2026-11-11, when ducking left the curve for a mode of its own), each measured by the preset report under every kit's kick on every beat at 120 BPM. Sidechain Duck turns a sound down gently under whatever its key plays, a voice or every drum (its key the whole sound): from -24 dB, 7.5 dB down in 5 ms, held as long as the key stays over that, and back in a straight line over 300 ms. Under a kick it goes 7.5 dB down and is back within 3 dB in 280 to 470 ms after a short one (the Dark Synthwave, Rhythm Box, Synthwave, Tight and Trap kits'); under a long, booming one (the 808's, 909's, House, Lo-Fi, Punchy and Wide kits') it stays down through the beat, as a ducker follows its key and those ring over -24 dB that long. Sidechain Pump makes dance music's pump, keyed by the kick's attack rather than its body (its key's low cut at 500 Hz, which the click of every kit's kick passes and its body doesn't), so that the duck is as long under any kick, however long it rings: from -28 dB, 10.5 dB down in 1 ms, held 40 ms from the hit and back in a straight line over 230 ms, as the guides set it (an attack of about 1 ms, a release of 80 to 250 ms, about 10 dB for a heavy duck): 10.5 dB down under every kit's kick (9 dB under the 808's, whose attack is soft) and back within 3 dB in 190 to 200 ms. Hearing the attack, it wants the kick alone as its key, a kick's track or a pad on a track of its own, as a "ghost" kick, muted, still is (a muted track still feeds a sidechain): a whole kit on one track would duck it at every snare and hat too. Sidechain Full Pump and Sidechain Deep Pump are the pumps of the original Exodus's two buses, measured on its recording, which the Exodus example song uses: keyed by the kick's attack in the same way, from -28 dB, down in 10 ms, held from the hit and back in a straight line, Full Pump 33.9 dB down, held 40 ms and back over 207 ms, Deep Pump 28.1 dB down, held 46 ms and back over 180 ms. A test plays each under Exodus's kick (the Dark Synthwave Kit's at velocity 100, at 114 BPM) and measures a tone's level in 2 ms windows against the original's: Full Pump's deepest -33.9 dB at 23 ms and back within 12, 6, 3 and 1 dB at 175, 209, 226 and 244 ms, Deep Pump's -28.1 dB at 22 ms and back at 149, 189, 206 and 220 ms; they come within 0.33 and 0.12 dB RMS of those. The original reached its deepest over 20 ms after the kick; theirs go down over 10 ms, so that the softer attacks of other kits' kicks still take them all the way down, and since 2026-11-12 they are all the way down as the kick plays, as the compressor looks ahead by its attack while it ducks, so that a note that starts with the kick doesn't click (its first milliseconds slipped through before). Under every kit's kick they go as deep (29 and 24 dB under the 808's) and are back within 3 dB at 230 and 210 ms.

**The EQ's dynamic and mid-side presets** (since step S6.4, `src/devices/eq/Equalizer.h`), each one band of an EQ otherwise flat: in Dynamic, bands whose gain moves only while their part of the sound is loud, De-Ess (a bell at 7 kHz, Q 2, Dynamic -8 dB from a Threshold of -32 dB), Tame Harshness (a bell at 3 kHz, Q 1.4, Dynamic -6 dB from -30 dB) and Boom Control (a low shelf at 120 Hz, Dynamic -6 dB from -13 dB, which a bass at -18 dBFS RMS stays under, its boomy notes over); in Mid-Side, bands on a part of the stereo image, Wider Top (a high shelf on the Side, +3 dB at 8 kHz), Mono Bass Cleanup (a 24 dB low cut on the Side at 120 Hz, which leaves the bass in the middle) and Centre Presence (a bell on the Mid at 3 kHz, +2 dB). The dynamic ones leave a sound that doesn't reach their Threshold as it is, and Wider Top and Mono Bass Cleanup a sound that is the same on both sides.

**The gate and transient presets** (the compressor's curve drawn as a gate, and its Transient mode; roadmap step S6.3).
A gate closes as the compressor's gain goes down, at its Attack, and opens as it comes back, at its Release; its Hold
keeps it open that long after the sound falls under the curve's step, so that it doesn't chatter on a drum's ringing
tail. Attack and Release reach down to a tenth of a millisecond. Drum Gate gates under -40 dB, opens in half a millisecond, 5 ms
ahead (its Look-Ahead), so that a hit's start passes whole (its first 2 ms within a decibel of how they came, bleed at
-45 dBFS before it more than 30 dB down: a test plays it), holds 60 ms and closes in half a millisecond, for a tom or a
kick picked up by other drums' microphones. Noise Gate gates under -50 dB, opens in a millisecond and closes slowly,
over 150 ms (held 20 ms), for hiss and hum between a voice's or a guitar's phrases. Gated Snare is the eighties' gated
reverb's shape: under -30 dB, opening in half a millisecond 5 ms ahead, held 250 ms, then shut in a tenth of a
millisecond.
More Punch turns each hit's attack up 7.5 dB (Punch 50 %); Tighter Tail turns what follows each hit down 11 dB
(Sustain -60 %, Speed 15 ms), for a roomy drum or a ringing tom. None turns the level of a steady sound, so none is
leveled.

**The saturator presets**, by how much each colours a sine (the preset report's total harmonic distortion at -18, -12 and -6 dBFS): Subtle Drive 0.3, 1.1 and 4 % (soft clipping at 4 dB of drive; until 2026-11-09 at 0 dB, which left a sound at -18 dBFS almost untouched), Warm Tape 1.4, 2.6 and 5 %, Tube Glow 1.8, 3.5 and 6 %, and Hard Clip and Wave Fold, clean until they clip or fold hard. Two use what roadmap step S6.6 added: Bit Crush, Crush at +12 dB of drive (6 bits), and Bass Warmth, its Band on below 200 Hz driven 9 dB into Tube, which warms a bass's lows and leaves its top as it came. The five from before play exactly as they did: S6.6 added Colour, Band, From, To and Quality at their defaults, which change nothing (a test played each against the saturator as it was, within 129 dB).

**The exciter presets** (the Saturator's Exciter kind, since roadmap step S6.6 merged the Exciter into the Saturator's Band): Saturated Highs was fitted to the Calf Exciter on Exodus's ADDsynth arpeggio (scope 2.1 kHz, harmonics 8.07, amount 1.44, blend 0, in the bright state that LMMS 1.2.2 renders and the recording plays) on the Exciter's renders: from 2.1 kHz, Drive 16 dB, Colour 35 %, Amount 180 %. The others are for what producers reach for an exciter for, each gentle on first load: Vocal Air, Snare Crack, Drum Bus Snap, Acoustic Presence, Synth Bite, Top End Restore (new highs for a dull sound) and Gentle Sheen (for a master). Each was converted from the Exciter's settings (Frequency, Drive, Colour, Amount, High Cut, Mix) to the Saturator's: Band on, From the Frequency, To the High Cut, Soft, Colour as it was, Auto Gain on, Tone 0, Mix as it was, Output 20 log10 (1 + Amount) dB (the same lift of the band), and the Drive lower, so that the harmonics come out as loud (the band is lifted by 1 + Amount where the Exciter added Amount times its harmonics); then the Drive, and From by up to a third of an octave, were tuned until the two devices played a fixed signal (pink noise at -18 dB RMS with sines at 2 and 6 kHz at -12 dB, stereo, 4 s) alike in every third-octave band from 100 Hz to 16 kHz: six within 0.3 to 1.8 dB in every band, Saturated Highs within 2.24 dB and Top End Restore within 2.51 dB, each at its worst in a band beside From, where From moved a third of an octave up for most of them (the table is on the [Saturator](design/devices/saturator.md)'s page). The Exciter's band fell 48 dB an octave and the Saturator's falls 24, so the bands beside From are where they differ most. Vocal Air, Acoustic Presence, Snare Crack and Synth Bite then took the conversion's Drive back (tuned down to match the bands' levels, the first two lifted their band with next to no harmonics), and Acoustic Presence a Tone of -10 %, which takes down the harmonics its curve makes above its To.

**Age's presets** (`src/devices/age/Age.h`; until 2026-11-18 the Vinyl's, which Age's record plays as it did, each preset naming the record as its medium, every part on and the tape's own at nothing): Warped Record is the VyNil effect on Exodus's arpeggio bus (year 1980.22, 33.02 rpm, warp 1, no crackle, no wear), fitted on Phazite's renders of test signals against LMMS 1.2.2's, measured alike: Wow 8.8 cents at 1.82 Hz (VyNil's code turns 60 / rpm times a second), Low Cut 145 Hz, High Cut 5 kHz, Distortion 8 %, Width 84 %, Hiss -48 dB, no crackle. Its wow is VyNil's within a tenth of a cent (8.82 and 2.21 cents at its rate and twice it, against 8.79 and 2.15; -13.4 to +4.7 cents against -13.5 to +4.9; 6.44 cents RMS against 6.41), its band within 1.3 dB from 63 Hz to 16 kHz, its hiss -51.2 dBFS RMS against -51.3; on the arpeggio's bus it narrows the sides 1.5 dB as VyNil does and takes 7.9 dB off 8 kHz against 8.0, but 0.67 dB off overall against 1.05. It leaves out VyNil's high-pass on the middle alone, its mono hiss, its third harmonic of the whole sound and its subsonic thumps. The others are for what producers reach for a vinyl effect for, each standing alone: Worn Record, Fifties Mono (a mono LP), Old 78 (wow at 1.3 Hz, a band of 250 Hz to 4.5 kHz, mono, a loud surface), Clean Pressing, Dusty Lo-Fi (dense crackle under a beat), Seasick Warp (20 cents at 0.3 Hz), Broken Player and Subtle Warmth (for a master, no noise). The tapes (Tapes) are each a machine as its name says: Clean Reel, a studio's reel-to-reel (a little drive, a 1.5 dB head bump, hiss at -72 dB, its wow and flutter a fraction of a cent, its band 25 Hz to 18 kHz); Warm Cassette (3 cents of wow, a little flutter, its drive at 40 % and a 2.5 dB head bump, hiss at -60 dB, a rare dropout, a 12 kHz top); Worn Cassette (7 cents of wow, 3.5 of flutter, dropouts at 30 %, hiss at -52 dB, a 9 kHz top); Chewed Tape (a deep, uneven wow of 18 cents at 0.4 Hz, dropouts at 70 %, a 7 kHz top); and Tape Saturation, the Drive part alone (60 %, a 2 dB head bump), every other part off, for a drum bus.

**The Stereo presets** (`src/devices/stereo/Stereo.h`): Checks are one switch each, for checking or mending a mix: Mono Check (Mono), Swap Sides, DC Filter, and Mono Bass (the bass under 150 Hz in the middle). Width widens or narrows: Wide Stereo (150 %, its bass kept in the middle), Narrow Image (50 %) and Mono to Stereo (Spread at 60 %, for a mono sound). Each leaves the level as it is.

**The delay's presets** (`src/devices/delay/Delay.h`; 2026-11-18, roadmap step S6.6): the six from before (Ambient Trails, Dotted Eighth, Ping-Pong Eighths, Quarter Echo, Dub Echo, Slapback Echo) play as they did, the parameters added at their defaults. Clean Quarter is a quarter-note echo in the Clean character, its top open to 12 kHz; Wide Slap two slaps without feedback, 90 ms on the left and 130 on the right (Link off), which widen a mono sound as a doubled slapback does; Dotted and Straight a dotted eighth on the left and a quarter on the right, so that the two make a rhythm between them; Wobbly Tape a dark quarter-note tape echo whose time wavers (Mod Depth 35 % at 0.8 Hz); Ducked Throw a long half-note ping-pong, ducked 70 % so that its echoes come up in the gaps between phrases, as producers throw a vocal; Dub Swell a dotted quarter in Tape at a Feedback of 97 %, its cuts narrow (400 Hz to 2.2 kHz), whose echoes ring on long and die away darker and thinner; turning its Feedback past 100 % while it plays is the dub move, the echoes swelling until the tape holds them, and back under 100 % they fall again (a preset left past 100 % would ring on after the song stops); and Chorus Echo a 20 ms echo whose time swings 1.8 ms either way at 0.7 Hz, a pitch swing of about 14 cents, a chorus with a little feedback. Each Mix is what the effect wants as an insert, from 20 % to 40 %.

**Sounds made from their spectra** (the sampler's Wide Saw, made by `src/devices/dsp/SpectralLoop.h`, Paul Nasca's PADsynth algorithm): a saw's harmonics (1/n) of A3, each spread into a Gaussian band with a power standard deviation of 4.8 cents, the same in cents at every harmonic, with random phases from a fixed seed, made into one seamless 5.5 s loop by one inverse FFT; the right side is the loop from half-way round, so the two sides are unrelated. It is the saw ensemble of ZynAddSubFX's PADsynth as XCALIBUR's Exodus plays it, without the beating of detuned oscillators. Its presets loop the whole sound from a random place at each note (Start Random 100 %): Wide Saw Pluck, Exodus's glassy pluck: a 12 dB low-pass at 5 kHz at velocity 100, 0.76 octave darker at 50 (Filter Velocity 48 %, ZynAddSubFX's law at the song's settings), and an attack and a decay to silence that halve with each octave up (Key Time 100 %; 19 and 272 ms at A4), within 1.2 dB RMS of the original's envelopes on the song's ten keys; Wide Saw Pad, which opens over 2.5 s and breathes with a slow LFO on its low-pass; and Wide Saw Strings, a string machine's ensemble. Every note differs by about 1.7 dB, so each is leveled on the worst of 200 to 300 draws: the tests' chord stays at or under -3 dBFS whatever it draws.

**Synthwave's bass and arpeggio** (the synth's, after what its producers' patches and the tutorials say, the research of 2026-11-09: Kavinsky's Nightcall bass as Reverb Machine takes it apart, Roland's and Syntorial's Juno and Moroder bass recipes, the Jupiter- and Prophet-style arps of MusicRadar and Syntorial, Eight Fifteen's and ModeAudio's octave copies): Synthwave Bass, a sustained two-saw bass an octave apart with its own sine sub, its filter near 1 kHz, made to be pumped by a sidechain, as every published synthwave bass carries its sub; Rolling Bass, the Juno's and Moroder's eighth- and sixteenth-note bass, two saws a few cents apart over a square sub, its filter opened and closed by a short envelope on each note; and Synthwave Arp, a bright, detuned saw pluck that doubles itself an octave up, its filter closing on each note. Each follows velocity and the mod wheel to its filter.

**The synth's unison stack and drift** (2026-11-10), besides Octave Supersaw Pluck: Fifth Lead, a power chord from one key, mono, as rave and trance leads play; Drifting Pad, two tables an octave apart whose copies each drift up to 30 cents, so that a held chord never sits still; and Flute Organ, an organ's flute stops at 8', 4' and 2', a pipe each, a few cents off each other, with a breath of noise, and no velocity, as organs have none.

**Synths** (Phazite's synth, after the 56 Juno-60 factory patches as the Hera emulator reads them, Sequential's own Prophet-5 recipes, the Alpha Juno's factory patch sheet, Open303's model of the TB-303, Adam Szabo's analysis of the JP-8000's supersaw, and Gordon Reid's Synth Secrets): Acid Bass glides (60 ms) only into a note played over a held one, as the 303 slides; DCO Strings and PWM Pad sweep a pulse's width with the LFO, PWM Pad's coming in over 1.5 s as the Juno's LFO delay brings it; Sync Lead sweeps a hard-synced oscillator with the envelope that opens its filter, as the Prophet-5's poly-mod does; Ladder Bass, Pedal Bass, Fat Lead and Scream Lead drive the filter, as a Minimoog's and an MS-20's do; Poly Pad, Poly Brass and the pads drift a little, as analog voices do. The chorus several of these want is an effect in Phazite, Motion, put after the synth.

**The fifty that came from a subtractive synth** (2026-11-18, roadmap step S6.7, which made Phazite's two synths one): Phazite had a subtractive synth beside this one until then, and its fifty presets were measured onto this synth's engine, keeping their names and kinds (the table-sync lead this synth had as Sync Lead is Digital Sync Lead, Sync Lead the Prophet-style one): the basses Acid Bass, DCO Bass, Ladder Bass, Mono Bass, Octave Bass, Pedal Bass, Pluck Bass, Reese Bass, Retro Bass, Sine 808 and Sub Bass; the brass Analog Brass, Brass Stab, Cinematic Brass and Poly Brass; the FX Noise Downlifter, Noise Riser, Noise Sweep and Zap; the keys Drawbar Organ, Poly Keys, Pulse Organ and Soft E-Piano; the leads Chip Lead, Duo Lead, Fat Lead, Glide Lead, Scream Lead, Square Lead, Supersaw Lead, Sweep Lead and Sync Lead; the pads Choir Pad, Dark Pad, Drone, PWM Pad, Poly Pad and Warm Pad; the percussion Electronic Tom and Sine Kick; the plucks Bright Pluck, Chord Stab, House Stab, Lo-Fi Pluck, Mallet Pluck, Square Pluck and Trance Pluck; and the strings DCO Strings, String Machine and String Pad. ## Leveling

A preset's level is set so that switching presets doesn't jump in loudness, and so that nothing clips, as the libraries users praise do and as users ask of the ones they don't (Diva's and SynthMaster's presets were re-leveled after complaints):
- **The loudness of one note at velocity 100,** its loudest momentary reading (EBU R128, 400 ms), aims at about -16 LUFS for the sustained kinds (basses, keys, brass, winds), -15 for leads, -17 for pads, strings and chords, and -19 to -20 for mallets, percussion and effects, whose short notes a 400 ms reading hears quieter than the ear does. Plucks sit lower, -22 to -25, where the chord's peak below holds their bright attacks. A new instrument plays at about -14 to -16.
- **Nothing peaks above -1 dBFS:** one note, and for a preset that plays chords (polyphonic, and not a bass, a drum or an effect) the four-note chord the preset tests play. Presets were leveled to -3 dBFS, for room, and the peak comes first: a preset whose chord reaches -3 dBFS stays there, though its note be quieter than its kind's aim (the supersaws, brass and electric pianos, whose chords peak high).
- **A kit's pads are balanced as a kit:** the kick its loudest, at about -16 LUFS in every kit so that switching kits doesn't jump, the snare and clap 3 to 5 LU under it, toms and percussion 5 to 8, hats and cymbals 8 to 11 (the House, Lo-Fi, Punchy, Tight and Wide kits' hats sat 18 to 23 LU under their kicks and their toms 2 to 4 until 2026-11-09).
- **The example songs keep their mix:** where a preset they use was re-leveled, the song's track is turned the other way.
- **At 48 kHz:** presets are leveled, and their tests play them, at 48 kHz. What plays differently at other rates is kept as it is there, and moves at the others: the synths' noise (`src/devices/dsp/Noise.h`) is the random numbers themselves at 48 kHz, as it was when Noise Riser, Noise Downlifter, Noise Sweep and the other presets with noise were leveled, and is as loud for each hertz at every other rate (0.37 dB down at 44.1 kHz, where the same noise spreads over a narrower band).

`tests/FactoryPresetTests.cpp` checks the peaks, and that each preset's loudness is within 12 LU of its instrument's median.

## Checking them

`tests/PresetReport.cpp` measures every instrument preset and writes WAV files to listen to. ctest skips it; run it from a `windows-profile` build:

```
$env:PHAZITE_PRESET_REPORT = "<a folder>"
build\windows-profile\phazite_tests_artefacts\RelWithDebInfo\phazite_tests.exe --test-suite=preset-report --no-skip
```

`PHAZITE_PRESET_FILTER` limits it to presets whose path holds a text ("Synth/Bass"). The folder gets `report.md`: for each preset, its peak, loudness, attack, decay, release, brightness (spectral centroid) at 30 ms, 250 ms and 1 s, pitch against the note played, stereo width, what velocity does to its level and brightness, and its chord's peak; for each kit's pads, their peak, loudness, length, brightness and pitch; and for each sidechain preset, how far and how long it turns a steady sound down under each kit's kick on every beat at 120 BPM; and for each saturator preset, its harmonic distortion on a sine at three levels, and for each with its Band on (the exciter presets among them), how far it lifts each octave from 2 to 16 kHz of pink noise at three levels; for each EQ preset, its response at the centres of the third-octave bands; and for each Age preset, how far it swings a sine's pitch, what its band does to pink noise, how far it narrows the sides, its distortion of a sine at 1 and 8 kHz, its hiss's level and its crackles. Beside it, a WAV of each preset's note and of a phrase of its kind (a bass line, a melody, chords, an arpeggio, a beat), and of a pad pumping under each kit's kick. The 2026-11-08 pass used it to find what was wrong before it was fixed: the Square Lead fading out and in each 1.3 s (two squares 5 cents apart at equal levels), the Comb Pluck 15 dB quieter than the rest, the 808 kit's snare ringing three times as long as an 808's and its clap's noise centred near 5 kHz where an 808's peaks at 1 kHz, its kick sweeping as a 909's does, and its hats full of noise an 808 hasn't, no Synth preset brighter for a harder note, and three FM basses an octave below the note played.
